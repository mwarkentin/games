## 2026-11-11

- Winning a war ends with SPOILS OF WAR!: the whole pot bursts out into a
  fan, the face-down cards turn over one by one, a counter tallies what you
  gained (the others' cards; your own just come back) and a war report lists
  the haul. It takes its time, running on into the next battle until you've
  read it (in a slow game, until you tap), then the cards fly onto your loot
  pile. Double and triple wars get a bigger show, someone else's war gets a strip
  with their haul and what you lost to it, and any tap sends it off early.
- As a war starts, the card you tied with stays where it lies, and the
  face-down cards land on it, instead of it being picked up and slammed
  down again.

## 2026-11-09

- Picking up your winnings is a moment of its own: the pile sweeps into a
  neat stack, turns over backs up, and tucks in under your deck, which lifts
  and settles thicker as its count ticks up, and riffles when winnings are
  shuffled back in.
  Everyone else's pickups show on their tiles too, and it never slows a slam.
- Against bots, cards follow their deck: with one bot every card is in the
  theme you picked, and with more each extra deck gets a theme of its own,
  so you can see whose deck a card came from wherever it goes. The deck
  picker's note says which deck is which.
- Commentary can be Quiet, on the menu or the pause screen: the captions
  just say what happened, with no jokes, win odds or science of slamming.
  The animations stay the same.
- A second tap on Join or Start a room no longer connects you twice, which
  counted every battle twice and could pull you back into a room you'd left.
- Playing solo while a room was still connecting no longer gets interrupted
  by that room turning up, or giving up, later.
- Deal again keeps the results on screen until the next game is dealt: if it
  can't be, they stay put, and each game counts once in your history.
- Reconnecting… now shows over the lobby and menus too, and Deal and Deal
  again wait until you're back.
- Music and sound effects stay quiet while the tab is hidden, and when you
  come back the music matches the screen: the end-of-game tune doesn't play
  again, and the results don't get battle music.
- Pausing a solo game stops everything until you're back: the next battle,
  the bots, the joker, and a war you'd started, which used to be slammed for
  you.
- Space and Enter press the button you're on instead of slamming, and Escape
  closes the power-ups on the menu too.
- Skipping to the end just as a battle was played no longer leaves it out of
  the battle log.

## 2026-11-08

- In a room, Clean up shows on everyone's screen: at a table of up to four,
  the others see your winnings squared up behind your card too.
- When jokers tie and start a war, the lights go out: two glowing eyes open
  in the dark and look around, a grin appears, and a lantern lights up a giant
  pixel jester cackling until the war opens. A joker war waits a moment longer
  to open than other wars, so the show has time. If that war ties on jokers
  again, its eyes turn red and more eyes open in the dark.
- Face cards and jokers have pixel art drawn for each deck in place of emoji:
  a juggling knave, a queen with a rose, a bearded king and a jester in
  Classic, glowing signs in Neon, a little alien, Princess Plum, a jolly king
  and a jester in 8-bit, a shield, a princess, a prince and masks in Royal, a
  rocket, an alien, a flying saucer and a comet in Space, the undead in
  Spooky, and sweets in Candy.
- Cards land face up as soon as they're played: yours the moment you slam
  it, everyone else's the moment they slam theirs, bots' too. Face-down war
  cards stay face down, and the winner still comes when everyone has played.
- Slow games, with no clock: pick one in the menu, or as host in a room's
  lobby. The table waits until everyone has played, and nobody's card flops
  over by itself. In a room, everyone sees who the table is waiting for and
  can give them a nudge, which shakes their deck; after a long wait the host
  can flip their card over for them.
- After a war, every card in the winner's loot pile is face up, the ones
  laid face down too.
- The cards you win stay beside your deck instead of vanishing into it, and
  your deck shows how many cards are left in it. Pick up puts your winnings
  under your deck in the order you won them. When your deck runs out, pick
  them up to keep playing (tap your deck or Pick up). On Auto, they come up
  by themselves.
- Winnings can be shuffled on their way back instead, as plenty of people
  play: pick it in the menu, or as host in a room's lobby.
- At a table of up to six, everyone else's tile shows their deck, with its
  count in the corner, and their loot pile, with its count, and the cards
  they play land on the table in the middle. A pot you win only counts once
  its cards have landed on your loot pile.
- Bots clean up their loot piles every so often, squaring them into a neat
  stack while they wait, the way you can with Clean up. A broom pops up when
  they do, and when someone in a room cleans up theirs.
- Every deck is as thick as it's full, and thins as it runs down.
- In a war, the cards everyone else lays face down fan out messily from
  under their card on the table, one for each card, so you can count them
  as they go down. In a double war or more, the cards that tied stay in
  view too, face up among them, in your pile as well as everyone else's.
- In a war, all your cards go on one pile until it's settled: the card you
  tied with, the ones you lay face down, and the card that decides it on top.

## 2026-11-07

## 2026-11-05

- Music: five public-domain pieces played on the chip voices of an old game
  console. The Entertainer plays in the menu, the Thunder and Lightning polka
  at the table, and In the Hall of the Mountain King when a war breaks out,
  getting faster with a double or triple war. Winning gets the end of The
  Stars and Stripes Forever, and losing gets a funeral drum. Turn it off with
  the note beside the sound button.
- Seven deck themes: Classic, Neon, 8-bit, Royal, Space, Spooky and Candy,
  each with its own card backs, faces and face-card pictures. Pick one from
  the menu, or from the pause menu mid-game. Everyone sees their own deck,
  even in a room.
- Play with friends: start a room from the menu and share its four-character
  code or invite link. The host can fill empty seats with bots, and anyone
  who arrives late watches until the next game.

## 2026-11-02

- War: Slam Edition is here. It's the card game War, with slams that go from
  "boop" to "COSMIC SLAM" and eleven power-ups to unlock, none of which change
  the outcome. Play against up to 49 bots, in a game of 20, 60 or 150 battles
  or in total war to the last card.
- A live win probability under the battle counter, which slams never move,
  and a chart of your chances at the end of the game.
