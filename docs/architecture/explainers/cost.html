    Where the numbers come from

    Each player-hour makes about 30 Worker requests (about 10 sockets opened while roaming, and world map reads), 106 Durable Object requests (those sockets and about 1,900 incoming messages, which count a twentieth each; outgoing ones are free) and 0.35 GB-s of Durable Object time, about 0.8 ms of a sector’s time per player each second. Those come from the survey’s design and a 700-player load test.

    Why it’s cheap

    No sector holds a timer while it waits, so between messages it hibernates and costs nothing, and positions go out once a second however many people move. Past the included allowances a thousand player-hours cost about 3 cents, and the survey takes at most 25,000 people at once, about $0.73 an hour.

    Not in the curve

    The list of surveyors and emotes add about $1.50 a day at 10,000 online, and guilds about $0.30. The survey’s log stays inside the included allowances at expected sizes; with every sector charting nonstop it raises the worst case from about $0.75 to $0.92 an hour.

    For comparison

    One Million Checkboards cost about $850 to run, on 8 VMs, Redis and bandwidth; One Million Chessboards, a few hundred dollars. With nobody playing, the survey costs the $5 plan.
